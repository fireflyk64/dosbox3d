# The TIE Fighter demo at 640x400

The user, 2026-11-22: "We want to make the tie fighter demo also 640x480 so that people don't assume the full game is
also trapped at 320x200 which should increase their chance of considering the full GoG version", in three steps: a
larger framebuffer (or one off canvas in DOSBox), the cockpit's edges at the higher resolution ("most of the items are
lines so they should be easy to outpaint and cut inward"), and the polygon renderer at the full resolution ("ideally
... all in dos, otherwise the polygon draw will have to be hooked and drawn in dosbox"). Then: "it can also be 640x400
if it's easier to manage"; "stars will need special drawing making them 1 pixel instead of the 2x2 ... so they don't
look like smudges on the lens"; "the distance culling (less than one px) will have to be adjusted to draw things
farther away so they don't pop in"; and, of the speed, "could we run the polygon renderer outside as an optimized wasm
routine?"

Branch `tie640`, worktree `~/dev/tie640`. The code: src/cpu/wcnet_tie640.cpp (and what the VGA's side calls,
src/hardware/vga_memory.cpp, vga_draw.cpp). docs/tiedemo.md is the demo's own guide (the program in memory, the
session).

## 1. What the demo does, and why its own picture cannot grow

* TIEMISS.EXE has no back buffer. Its renderer (image paragraph 1CCA, hand-written) turns the scene's polygons into
  runs for each scanline, compares each row's runs with the frame before's, and writes only the changed runs straight
  into the VGA's mode 13h memory; the cockpit is on the screen already, and a mask of runs keeps the scene off it.
* A run's length is a byte (two for 256 and more), the edge tables have a row for each of 200 lines, the row offsets
  are a table of 320s, and a 640x400 picture is 256,000 bytes, four real-mode segments. So the picture is made by the
  emulator, and the game keeps its 320x200.
* The 3D window is 320x189 (the cockpit view and the view without a cockpit; the message line is the rows below);
  the keypad's look-around views have a window of 240x154 at its own corner. The fine scene is twice the window:
  640x378.

## 2. The picture shown (wcnet_tie640.cpp)

While the demo's flight runs in mode 13h, the VGA's drawing is overridden (`VGA_SetOverride`) and the emulator shows
640x400 lines of its own at the VGA's frame rate. It is made of:

* **L**, the game's own 320x200 (the VGA's memory): the cockpit, the instruments, the brackets, the text, drawn by the
  game as before. Its window holds the background colour (DS:48BC, F7) wherever nothing is drawn over the scene:
  the scene itself is no longer drawn there.
* **W**, the window: the game's mask, read each frame (in the run tables' segment DS:D8A6 at DS:28B6: for each row a
  flag byte, top bit set when the row begins closed, then run lengths, closed and open in turn, 0 then a byte for
  256 and more).
* **H**, the scene at twice the window, drawn by the game's own renderer (section 3).

Every pixel of L becomes four. Inside the window a pixel still at the background colour shows H's four; anything the
game drew over the scene (the sight, a bracket, a letter) is doubled; outside the window the cockpit is doubled.

**The cockpit's edge** (`smooth_edge`). W's edge is a staircase of runs along a row or a column, each one step from
the next; the line it stands for passes through the runs' middles. So each run gives its last quarter towards a
one-pixel step half a pixel (one of the fine grid's) to the next run's level: the fine pixel there shows the other
side's picture (the scene cut in, or the neighbouring cockpit pixel painted out). The struts' edges are one-pixel
steps of the 640x400 at any slope. (Scale2x's corner rule, tried first, leaves a diagonal as coarse as it was: a
corner cut and the next filled is the same two-pixel staircase, moved.) The cockpit's own art inside the edge is the
320 art, doubled.

**The gunsight** (`find_islands`, `thin_overlay`). The game closes the sight in its mask and draws it itself: 320
art, whose one-pixel strokes were two wide over a scene at the finer grid (the user: "fix ... the reticle specifically
to be thinner"). The mask's closed patches that are no part of the cockpit (they reach no side of the window, none
wider than a quarter of it) are drawn as a picture of their own: each pixel one fine pixel at its cell's corner,
joined to its neighbours (right, below, across a diagonal when nothing else joins them) by the fine pixel between. A
one-pixel stroke is one fine pixel wide and as long as before; a filled patch stays filled; the scene shows through
the rest. `WCNET_T640_THINSIGHT=0`: doubled as before.

`WCNET_TIE640=0` keeps the game's own picture. The `` ` `` key (or Shift+`` ` ``: the page's voice on push to talk keeps plain
`` ` `` for talking) turns the 640x400 off and on in flight, as Privateer's and Armada's flips do (`tie640_flip`; the
picture follows at the next `tie640_tick`). `!shot=` writes the 640x400.

## 3. The scene: one pass of the game's upper half, a hand-written lower half

The renderer's lower half cannot hold a picture wider than 320 pixels (in its lists an x is 9 bits packed with the
light, a row a byte, the depth << 8 a word), and there is no list of polygons to take over: the game traces every
face straight into per-row lists of edge crossings. So (wcnet_tie640_onepass.cpp, the default, WCNET_T640_NATIVE=2):

* **The scene section runs once** (0090:0B1A..1069) with the projection's centre and scale doubled (the scale by
  code patches, `scale_code`) and the window 640x378 (DS:48C5.., written at 0090:0B36, put back at 1069). The window
  is the game's own 320x189 again around the sprites' and the backdrop's code (1CCA:DF00 / 0775 to A02A's end;
  0090:0B5C widens it again after the backdrop): that code is sized for the 320x189 buffer, and the game left the
  flight when it saw 640.
* **Capture.** Every call of the lower half's entries is taken down with what it was given and left at once:
  trace2_drawface (1CCA:53F3), trace2_drawscreencoords (4FF3), drawln2_tracelineedges (2BB0), the markings (1CFC,
  1FB0, 2070), each object's start and end (0AAB, 0B41), the scanline pass (85E9).
* **Replay.** For each quarter of the picture an image of the game's memory as that quarter's own pass would have
  it; the calls made again on it by hand-written tracers (wcnet_tie640_trace.cpp: the face tracer and all it calls,
  flat polygons, thick lines, the sprites' rows), the screen points moved by the quarter's corner; and what depends
  on the window worked out for the quarter: an edge's side of it, which objects and faces the game would draw there
  and their numbers, the shading state (DS:26DC: drawpol writes it only for a face not shaded, a cut face leaves
  7), a marking's colours.
* **Resolve.** A hand-written scanline pass (wcnet_tie640_native.cpp: the rows' crossings, an object's open faces,
  the heap of open objects and which is in front, the markings, the flat, buffer and shaded dither fills) writes
  each quarter into the 640 picture. A depth sort it needs of the game (draw_polydepthsort) is run by the
  interpreter and the quarter made again.
* **Sprites and the backdrop**: the game's own scaler (1CCA:A02A) is run ONCE a sprite at the game's own scale (the
  place and the scale halved) into a buffer of its own, and the sprite's box is put into each quarter's copy of the
  320x189 buffer twice the size, a pixel four; the row pass (A132) on each is the hand-written one. (Until
  2026-11-24 the scaler was run in each quarter at twice the scale, `WCNET_T640_SPR1X=0`: four runs a sprite of four
  times the pixels. The user, of the page: "unacceptably slow, especially when any kind of explosion renders";
  then: "I like the idea of drawing sprites with 2x2 pixels instead of upres since the original art is meant for
  lower scale anyway".)
  The scaler is run to its end inside the hook by the emulator's own interpreter, outside the emulated clock
  (`run_to_idle`: its return lands on a `jmp $` written at PSP:0060): the game's code as it is, at none of the
  frame's cycles (under an explosion it was most of them). What the scaler leaves in its own variables (DS:4320 on)
  is kept for each quarter: the scanline pass reads them through the game's bug at 9DFD.
* **The stars** (wcnet_tie640_stars.cpp): the game's routine is left at its entry; its 256 stars are plotted
  natively, one fine pixel each at twice the scale, colours lifted to 150% (WCNET_TIE640_STARGAIN).
* **Farther detail**: the depth a craft's detail level is picked by, and the depth in the three "smaller than a
  pixel" tests, are halved (code patches).

So the formats stay the game's own and the picture is, pixel for pixel, the one the game's own renderer draws when
its scene section is run four times, a zoomed quarter each (WCNET_T640_NATIVE=0; =1 the quarters with the
hand-written scanline pass). No code of the game's is translated by machine: the first fast version was a 3 MB
translation of the section (scripts/tie640-recompile.py makes it; build with -DWCNET_T640_RC), not kept.

Two of the game's own bugs are kept because pixels depend on them (the right-of-window edges' steppers without their
segment prefix, 1CCA:8D80; a flat object's component number read 80h too far, 9DFD).

## 4. Checked

`WCNET_T640_CHECK=3` draws every frame in quarters by the game's own code (with `WCNET_T640_FAST=0`: no object left
out of a quarter), puts the memory back, draws it in one pass and compares the pictures and the game's state.
`$S/tools/opall.sh`: nine scenes (fighter with fire and a kill, a crowd of craft, the look-around views, no cockpit,
a craft at the near plane 20 / 60 / 150 units and off axis, kills in the crowd), 440 to 1,000 frames each:

* 0 pixels in seven or more of the nine every run; the objects and craft data never differ.
* What is left, a frame in several hundred:
  * a sprite the GAME drew differently from one quarter's pass to the next (an explosion's turn is not the same
    in each of the reference's four passes: the check logs "sprite N differs" with what the scaler was given, and
    every difference of more than a few pixels comes with that line). The seam is the reference's; the one pass
    draws the sprite once.
  * a marking's polygon all in one row, 1 or 2 pixels a shade's step off (the game's bug 4: its crossings' x is
    what the tracer's call before left in EAX's high half, which the hand-written tracers do not give back).
* The hand-written tracers alone: `WCNET_T640_TRACECHECK=1`, each call against the game's own on a copy of the
  memory, byte for byte: 0 of some 600,000 calls differ. The scanline pass alone: `WCNET_T640_CHECK=1` / `=2`.
* Flown (2026-11-23): a death and the next launch, a mission won and the next mission (the demo's TIE fighter has
  no hyperdrive: "this craft is not equipped"), the look-around views, no cockpit.
* Two machines (scripts/tdmnet-sync.sh) and three (WCPLAYERS=3), all in one pass: 18 of 18 craft the same in every
  table.

## 5. Speed (30,000 cycles, the page's setting)

The game's own 320 picture: 59 frames a second (its cap) in empty space, 10 to 44 under fire at a fighter 400 units
ahead with its explosion. One pass, natively and on the page: 57 to 59 in empty space, with a fighter filling the
view, and under that fire and explosion; 36 to 43 on the page with nine craft held in view (the game's own upper
half for that many). The four quarters by the game's own code: 7 to 17.

In the browser the hooks run from the main loop and each watched place reached is a trip out of the CPU's core:
`wc::watch_at(seg, ip)` keeps other segments' code from tripping on a watched offset, and the one pass's places in
the renderer are dealt with in the core itself (`tie640_fast_trip`).

**Against the host's clock** (2026-11-24; the user: "the interesting number is the minimum framerate over 30 seconds
of gameplay"). The figures above are the game's frames by its own clock; the scaler's runs inside the hook pass no
emulated time, so they showed in none of them. `!wallfps` (and a line every 30 s of flight) counts the frames of
each second of the host's clock. On the page, a crowd of craft under fire with six kills: with the scaler run in
every quarter the fewest frames in a second were 25 and then 0, the emulator at 84% and 66% of real time, the
scaler's runs 10.1 s of 30; with one run a sprite 45 and 44, at 98% and 79% (the game's own 320x200 picture: 22).
Natively 57 at the fewest.
