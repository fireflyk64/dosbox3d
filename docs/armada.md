# Wing Commander: Armada in the browser

Open [DOSBox](https://fireflyk64.github.io/dosbox/?game=armada) and drop your GOG offline installer into **Game**. A zip of the installed DOS directory works too. Include `ARMADA.EXE`, `NETBIOS.EXE`, and the game data. `PG.EXE` and `PG.TRE` add Proving Grounds. Each pilot loads their own copy, which the browser keeps locally.

## Armada for two players

1. Pick **Wing Commander: Armada**. Host a two-player room and send its link or code. Once both players are in, the host presses **Fly** to start both copies.
2. In each game, skip the intro with **Esc**. Choose **Multi Player**, then **Net Play**. Press **Y** to accept the default group name and enter separate callsigns. Wait for the in-game lobby to finish initializing.
3. The host clicks **Create**. For the strategy game, enter **A**, then **C** for Confederation or **K** for Kilrathi. Press **Enter** after each answer. For an immediate dogfight, enter **B** and press **Enter**.
4. The guest picks the host's game in the in-game list and clicks **Join**. Once it shows two players, the host clicks **Play**.
5. In strategy mode, the host chooses the sector size. Both players click **Begin** to open their maps. Click **Execute** and confirm **Yes** when ready to end the turn. The game advances after both players confirm. In Battle, each pilot chooses a ship, clicks **Select**, then **Commit** to launch.

## Proving Grounds arena

1. Pick **Armada: Proving Grounds** in the same Game menu. Host a room for two to six players and share its code or link. Press **Fly** when everyone is in.
2. At the title, press **Space** if needed to show the menu. Choose **IPX Net Play** and enter separate callsigns. In the in-game lobby, the host clicks **Create**, enters **P**, and chooses an asteroid density. **N** starts an arena without asteroids. Press **Enter** after each answer.
3. Each guest selects the host's game and clicks **Join**. The host clicks **Play**. Each pilot picks a ship, clicks **Select**, and clicks **Commit**. **T** changes teams on the ship selection screen.

## Controls

- **Keyboard.** Steer with the arrow keys or numeric keypad. A tap turns a little; held, a key builds to the full turn in a third of a second, as in Wing Commander and Privateer. **Shift** with an arrow turns at full at once. **Space** fires guns, **Enter** fires missiles, **Tab** applies afterburners, and the keypad **+** and **−** change speed.
- **Mouse.** Click normally in the menus and on the strategy map (with **High resolution (2x)** the map shows one level out; the wheel up shows the game's own map, down one level out again). The arrow keys move the map. The game's own keys press its buttons (F1 Menu, F2 Move, F3 Catalog, F4 Dock, F5 Planet menu, F6 Move resources, F7 Information, F8 Execute; C and D are a dialog's Cancel and Done); **+** and **−** work the arrows of a dialog's top row (Move resources: a ton onto or off the ship; Dock: a fighter docked or undocked; a squadron's Move: one more going or staying); other rows by clicking. In flight, hold the right button and drag to steer, as a stick would: the further the drag from where the button went down, the faster the turn (64 px is the full turn of an arrow key held). Release the right button to center the steering. Press the left button while steering to fire guns. Dragging up raises the nose; tick **Invert mouse** to steer as a flight stick instead (dragging up, the nose goes down, the arrow keys' way).
- **Joystick or gamepad.** Connect the controller, press a button so the browser sees it, and pick its **Built in** entry under **Controller** before pressing **Fly**. No calibration is needed: the page writes the game's calibration file, and the emulator gives the game exact readings to go with it (see "The joystick" below). To recalibrate anyway, **Alt+O** opens Options from the main menu; **JOY 1** asks for the stick at the upper left, the lower right and the centre, each with the button (the game's own dead band of a tenth of each side comes back with it). [Origin's Armada Play Guide](https://download.wcnews.com/files/arma/Armada%20-%20Play%20Guide%20-%20Text.pdf)

- **High resolution (2x).** Tick it in the room to fly at 640×400 (your own screen only): the space is fine (ships, lasers, explosions, stars), the reticle and the target's box thin lines, the cockpit and displays the game's own. <kbd>`</kbd> flips it while flying. Off unless ticked.

## Network and checks

`ARMADA.EXE` uses the bundled Novell `NETBIOS.EXE` driver over IPX. The browser launcher enables DOSBox IPX for multiplayer and runs the driver before Armada. `PG.EXE` uses IPX directly. Each emulator has its own IPX node, and the room host forwards guest IPX datagrams over the LobbyLink data channels. Armada and Proving Grounds handle their own matchmaking, turns, and combat.

`scripts/armadanet-duo.sh` runs the native emulator pair over TCP. `scripts/web-armada.mjs` runs the browser build against a LobbyLink server. Set `PLAYWRIGHT_DIR` to a directory with Playwright installed, then pass the page URL, the lobby server URL, and the GOG installer. A fourth argument can pass the guest a zip instead. Set `MODE=strategy` or `MODE=arcade` for those routes. The script saves each player's game screens in `SHOT_DIR`.

## The arrow keys and the mouse (src/cpu/wcnet_armada.cpp)

Armada turns at full rate the moment an arrow key goes down, so a tap overshoots the target. Privateer had the same fault. Every flight frame the game builds its pilot's control packet. That packet is the few bytes it sends to the other machines, and every machine steers that pilot's ship from them. Byte 3 is the stick's left and right and byte 4 its up and down, each a 7-bit signed number; the joystick's reading goes in first. Then a loop over the key bindings checks the game's held-key table. Each held key bound to a stick action writes the stop at once: Down +30 in byte 4, Up −30, Right +30 in byte 3, Left −30 (Proving Grounds uses 20).

The emulator watches those four writes and the loop's end. At the end it writes the bytes again, ramped the way Wing Commander's and Privateer's arrows are. A held key starts at a ninth of the stop and adds a ninth every 37 ms, reaching the stop in a third of a second. Shift with the key gives the stop at once. Letting go, or pressing the opposite key, starts the ramp again. Each machine sends the packet after this rewrite, so every machine in a room flies the same ramped turn. Only the keys' writes change; a joystick's reading is the game's own. `WCNET_ARM_RAMP=0` keeps the game's original keys.

| Program | Code segment | Writes A, B, C, D | Loop end | Held keys | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ARMADA.EXE | 2584 | 14D0, 14EC, 1508, 1524 | 1689 | DS:8E56 | 30 |
| ARMADACD.EXE | 2587 | same | 1689 | DS:8ED6 | 30 |
| PG.EXE | 1B68 | 0E2A, 0E46, 0E62, 0E7E | 0FE3 | DS:7B1C | 20 |
| PGCD.EXE | 1B6C | same | 0FE3 | DS:7C2C | 20 |

Segments count from the load segment. When a program loads, the emulator checks the bytes at each of these places; a build that differs keeps the game's own keys.

Measured natively at 25,000 cycles (about 19 frames a second), holding Right in a solo Gauntlet flight with the ship stopped:

- **Stock game:** 30 from the first frame. A 0.1 s tap moved the view about 70 px in one frame, and a hold about 60 px per 0.1 s from the start.
- **Ramped:** 3, 6, 13, 16, 23, 26, then 30 from the seventh frame. A tap gave 3 and 6, about 12 px of view. A hold built through about 6, 12, 40 and 56 px per 0.1 s to the same full rate.
- **Proving Grounds** (two machines over TCP, in the arena): 2, 4, 8, 11, 15, 17, then 20, for both Right and Up.

When an arrow is let go, the log says how it ramped ("Armada: Right held 0.85 s, 16 frames, ramped +3 to +30"); `scripts/web-armada.mjs` checks that line for the guest's Right.

**The mouse.** Armada uses the mouse only in its menus. In flight, the page's right-button drag steers through the same place, as a stick would. The page sends the drag's offset from where the button went down (`wc_web_arm_steer`, −1 to 1 each way, right and down positive; 3 px dead, full at 64 px). At the loop's end the emulator writes it into the packet in proportion to the keys' stop, on any axis that no key and no joystick moves this frame. Letting go of the button centres it. The left button with the right fires the guns (Space). As with the keys, the packet carries the turn to the other machines. Measured natively with the `!armsteer=x:y` key-script aid: a quarter drag wrote 8 and turned the view about 12 px per 0.1 s, about a fifth of the full turn; a full drag wrote 30; an arrow pressed during a drag ramped its own axis while the drag held the other.

**The joystick.** The user, 2026-11-23: "Ideally the OS itself handles joystick calibration so the ingame calibration seems to be too much work for plyaers these days. Can we bypass that unless they want to *recalibrate*", and "we need to reduce the default deadzone in wing commander privateer and this to 4%". Origin's library reads the game port in `seg5876:003E` (ARMADA.EXE; the same code at `5879:003E` in ARMADACD.EXE, `53F7:0034` in PG.EXE, `5416:0042` in PGCD.EXE). It times each axis of both sticks against the PC's timer (port 40h, channel 0 latched) until the port's bit clears, and keeps the times (DS:5C7C A x, 5C7E A y, 5C80 B x, 5C82 B y), the buttons (5C84) and which axes answered (5C85: an axis that does not clear within 65,535 reads is dropped). The calibration (`JOYA.DAT`, loaded at the start when it is there, saved by JOY 1) is in those times: each axis's lowest, centre and highest reading, its 32 steps a side and each step's size, and a dead band of a tenth of each side around the centre (`seg57F4:0403`). A reading above the band is ceil((reading − top) / size) steps, below it floor((reading − bottom) / size); the control packet then counts −2..2 steps as none (`seg2584:0A0A`).

Timed, the readings depend on how fast the emulated CPU runs the loop on DOSBox's counting port (`timed=false`, the page's): measured natively with the old calibration, a stick at rest read 13 of the 32 steps left and down at the high-resolution picture's 75,000 cycles, and every reading jittered by a few steps. So while the page's stick is on the port (stick A, not B) the emulator answers the reader itself (`joy_read` in `wcnet_armada.cpp`): it puts exact readings where the reader keeps them and returns. 321 is the centre and 0 and 642 the ends. The page writes `JOYA.DAT` to match (`ARMADA_JOYA` in `web/gamefiles.js`; `JOYPORT=1 scripts/armadarun.sh` natively): steps of 10, the band 320..322, so the page's dead zone (4%) is the only one. A stick past it is step 3 at least, the first step the packet counts, and steps 3..32 share the rest of the travel equally, each reading in the middle of its step. Stick B's axes leave the mask, as the reader's own timeout would drop them. `WCNET_ARM_JOY=game` keeps the game's own reader.

Measured natively (`!joy=x:y`, `WCNET_ARM_PACKET=1`), the same at 320 x 200 and at 640 x 400: full stick ±32, half 17, a tenth 5, 1% 3, at rest 0, every frame. `scripts/web-armada-pad.mjs` (`HIRES=1` for 640 x 400) flies a mocked pad on the page with no calibration and checks those numbers in the packet.


## Twice the resolution (src/cpu/wcnet_armada_hires.cpp)

The user, 2026-11-22: "lets figure out how to double the screen x and y for armada. This is a polygon renderer so it should be scalable"; "It should be toggleable from the browser too"; "We will need to change clip distance so that sub pixel in 320 times will show up when size of a 640 pixel"; and, of the frame rate, "I want to make sure the uprez doesn't change game behaivor at all".

**How Armada draws a flight.** ARMADA.EXE runs in V86 mode under JEMM.OVL, Origin's own memory manager (16-bit protected mode at selector 18h maps EMS-like pages; a 32-bit helper at 98h copies pages and writes the changed dwords of a frame to mode 13h). Every flight frame the frame function (seg2B4B) draws into a 320x200 back buffer, a bitmap record (segment, row table, clip rectangle) at DS:[621A], in one section:

| Offset from the section's top (2B4B:061A) | What |
|---|---|
| +00..+0E | the buffer cleared to the frame's colour |
| +11 | the display list (DS:85A4) emptied |
| +25 | setup: every point projected through the view (DS:SI+8, copied to DS:83BA: centre x, y; scale x, y in 8.8), the list built |
| +3B | the list sorted by depth (a radix sort) |
| +51 | the list drawn: each entry's own draw (polygons through the library's filler, 22-byte corners already in pixels) |
| +57..+80 | an optional overlay, then the cockpit and instruments |

The CD build and both Proving Grounds programs have the same section at their own places (each found by its bytes and checked at the first frame; a program that differs keeps its own picture).

**The four quarters.** With the picture on, the section runs four times first, each with the view made a quarter's of a 640x400 picture (centre 2c less the quarter's corner, scale doubled, the clip rectangle likewise), and each time the buffer is kept as that quarter. Then it runs once exactly as the game runs it, its view and its state, so everything the frame leaves behind is the game's own. That pass's scene is kept, the buffer filled with colour FF (neither cockpit draws it), and the cockpit drawn over it as usual: where FF is left the 640x400 scene shows, anywhere else the game's pixel doubled. The buffer then gets the game's scene back under the cockpit, so the screen's memory holds exactly the game's frame. The picture is shown through the VGA override (Privateer's seam), from the first frame drawn so until the screen shows anything else (a menu, the end of a battle).

**What a draw changes is put back.** Some draws change their own object each time they run, which four extra passes would multiply: the star field moves its stars by the camera's motion (a resource of 12-byte positions), keeps its last camera place, and adjusts its count of stars by the frame rate; an explosion piece counts its frame on (+32). Before each pass after the first these are as they were before the frame, so only the game's own pass changes them. Found by comparing the whole emulated memory after passes 0, 1 and 2 (`!armside`) and by reading every kind of draw met (`WCARMHIRESDRAWS=1` logs each new one): what still differs between passes is the picture, per-vertex screen positions (rewritten by the game's pass), scratch, stacks, page tables and interrupt-time state (the music driver, the network, the timer).

**Drawn here in the quarters.** The far-off sprites (the sun, the planets) are drawn by the game at a fixed size: in the quarters each pixel is drawn 2x2. The library's turned-and-scaled shapes (explosions) are drawn here too, at the doubled scale and clipped to the quarter (the game's skipped one whose middle lay in another quarter: a straight cut down the picture).

**The draw range.** Armada draws nothing beyond DS:8646 (1,980 of its 5 m units, 9.9 km): a fighter comes in at 16 pixels wide. For the quarters it is 16 times as far (`WCARMHIRESFAR`), so ships come in when they are a few fine pixels; the game's own pass, targeting and the HUD keep the game's range.

**The frame rate is the game's.** Armada holds its own frame time: after each frame it waits for 375 units of its timer (about 58 ms here, 17.1 frames a second), and in a room the simulation's rate is the session's. The four quarters take about three times the scene's work, so while the picture is 640x400 the emulated CPU gets three times the cycles (`WCARMHIRESCYCLES`; the game on a faster PC, waiting longer), and that wait is skipped ahead 0.05 ms at a time instead of spun (less than its timer's unit: it ends on the same unit). Measured in a two-machine battle with a missile, guns and the kill: 17.1 frames a second throughout, the passes 18-20 ms of the 58.3 ms frame (27 at the explosion); on the page (headless Chrome here) 17.1 frames a second and 1.00 emulated second a second for both pilots, in Armada and in Proving Grounds.

**One level: the space** (the user, 2026-11-24, of the battles: "Lets focus on the middle mode where the ships are high resolution but the cockpit and reticle are not"; "I think for now we should remove the code to make the very high resolution displays as they are going very slow"; then "Basically we just need thin lines for target box and thin lines for the reticle--and at least the reticle is a static image"). The space is 640x400; the cockpit and the displays are the game's own pixels, doubled. Two things of the cockpit's stage are drawn at the fine pixels, into an overlay laid over the composed picture, the game's own call stepped over:

- *The target's box*: the library's line (bitmap record, x0, y0, x1, y1, colour) drawn on the space (a third of its pixels or more on the marker: the box's eight corner pieces, seg1D16:1B64..1C98; the reticle, drawn first, crosses them) is one fine pixel wide (src/cpu/wcnet_armada_art.cpp, Privateer's fine line). With the cockpit off (F1) the displays draw over the space too, but not with the line: they stay the game's.
- *The reticle, from an icon* (the user: "I wonder if we could have saved out reticle icons instead of runnign some post process"). Both sides' reticles are one shape file's, drawn by the library's shape in one colour at the view's middle (seg5F69:0008): the main one (shapes 0 and 1: a circle of radius 9, four spokes from 6 to 13 out with bars across at 13, 11, 9 and 7, half 2, 1, 2, 1 long) and a small one (shape 2: a dot in a diamond of radius 2, spokes to 4). Each has a hand-made icon of the same geometry at the fine pixels (a circle of 18, the rest lines), one fine pixel wide, about the fine pixel of the game's middle, in the call's colour. The game's shape is known by its outline (an FNV-1a hash of its size, anchor and lit pixels: nothing of the game's art is kept), looked up once a shape; any other is the game's own. `WCARMHIRESRETICLE=0`: the game's; `WCARMHIRESRETICLES=<dir>` saves each reticle met and its file's every shape.

Until 2026-11-24 there was a second level, *all*: every cockpit line fine, the reticle made into line art each frame's way (its pixels joined, its rings fitted), the target display's ship at the fine pixels and the window's edge smoothed; with the Kilrathi cockpit and the cockpit off it looked wrong (the user: "the shoklar and any F1 (cockpit off) views look really funky with that preprocesing on it"). It is gone (git history: c8390429 and before).

**Done on the host, not the emulated CPU** (the user, 2026-11-24: "the battles are super slow with the high resolution ... when I aim and the target is big things really slow down"; "the battle was slow in the browser with netplay enabled"; "We may need more ideas to offload computation to the host"). In the browser the emulated CPU is the cost (Chrome's profile of the page: the core and its memory accesses, nearly all of it; composing and the canvas barely show), so what counts is the instructions a frame really runs. Profiled close up (`WCNET_ARM_PROF`, by pass), three things are done here instead, each exactly as the game does it (checked: `=check` runs the game's own and compares every result; over 800,000 spans and 560,000 stars, none differed):

- *The texture mapper's span* (seg30DD:1387 `jmp word ss:[197E]` to its loop with a shading table, 138C, or without, 13B8; the CD build 30E0, Proving Grounds 25A9/25AD:138D with its tables at 17CC..): the ships are texture-mapped, a dozen instructions a pixel, and a ship close by in the quarters is four times the pixels. Here a pixel at a time as the loop does it (the texel at DS:SI unless FFh, through FS:[texel + table] with the table, to ES:DI; DX += AX and CX += BP, their carries choosing SI's step from the table of four), then on after the loop, whose registers and stack word are all popped or overwritten. In every pass; a page not in the host's memory leaves it to the game. `WCARMHIRESTEX=0|check|alt`.
- *The stars* (the user: "stars can also be drawn as 1x1 rather than 2x2 so it shouldn't increase the amount of work as before--just change the number of pixels drawn"): the field is drawn by the game in the first quarter only; its view is the 640x400 one less that quarter's corner, so each star's pixel call (seg29CA:0B4B: bitmap record, x, y, colour) gives its 640x400 place. In the other quarters the field's draw is stepped over and those stars put in the quarter's pixels at the field's place in the display list (what is drawn later still covers them). The game's own pass draws the field as always: it moves the stars. A star's colour is its distance's square (seg40B2:0865: the sum of the squares, no root: the user asked "can we square the distance and work in that squared space instead of square-rooting everything": the game already does) looked up in a falling table a word at a time (29CA:0B15): done here in every pass. `WCARMHIRESSTARS=0|check`.
- *The textured polygon's whole routine* (seg30DD:0EA2; the CD build 30E0, Proving Grounds 25A9/25AD:0EA8; the four builds' routines one, its words for the tables and its loops' places apart, known by a hash): with the span done here the rest of it was the work left, a sixth of a close-up frame: each corner's clip code and the top corner, each edge's three slopes (idivs), and for each row its span's ends, clip and step table (two idivs). Here the whole routine is done as the emulated CPU does it, instruction by instruction: the same 32-bit registers and flags at its return, every byte it writes on its stack (its frame and pushes) and in ss:[197E..198D], in the same order, and every pixel; the game goes on at its return. Nothing is guessed: before anything is written every page it reads or writes is looked up in the page tables as the CPU would at user level (nothing set in them), and the game's own routine does any call where one would fault, the corners wrap their segment, there are none, or the stack is not dword-aligned (the routine then copies its arguments and calls itself aligned: that call is done here). On the way, at anything the CPU would fault on (an idiv's overflow), a walk round the corners that would never end, a span whose pixels would land on what the routine keeps (its stack, ss:[197E..], its code, the page tables) or a texel or pixel not reachable, the emulated CPU is given the state exactly there (registers, flags, that instruction) and goes on from it. `WCARMHIRESPOLY=0|check|alt` (`check`: each polygon worked out aside, then the game's routine run with interrupts held off and the two compared at its retf: registers, flags, every byte of the bitmap's segment, of the stack and of the tables; 726,000 polygons on both machines close up, none differed. Without interrupts held off, four differed, each time the routine's saved DI/SI/DS just under its SP after it had popped them: a timer interrupt's return frame landing between its `pop ds` and its `retf`, as on any machine). Close up (`=alt`, the polygons on and off every 2 s), the machine with the other's ship large in view: 22.1-22.4 ms of emulated work a frame → 19.7-20.0 (the worst frame 23.2-23.9 → 22.1-22.7).
- *The game's own pass draws no textured polygon* (the user: "skipping pixel draw would be good"): while the picture is 640x400 that pass's scene is never seen (the 640x400 one is shown, the cockpit drawn over the marker), so the textured polygon's routine (seg30DD:0EA2; Proving Grounds 0EA8) is stepped over there, all its work; it changes only the picture and its own scratch, and its callers ignore AX. The stars, explosions and the rest still draw in that pass: they move their own objects. `WCARMHIRESOWN=draw`.
- *The room's wait* (seg23E6:0616..067E: every player's poll until each has its packets for the frame): the machine waiting for the other spun there at three times the cycles, which in a browser is real work; now each round of it is skipped ahead as the frame's wait is (while the picture is 640x400). `WCARMHIRESNET=0`.

Measured (two machines, Battle, both stopped about 200 m apart facing each other, `!armstopnear=12000`; `!armhost=0|1` switches all three within the run, each half 10 s): the passes 29 ms of emulated time a frame → 17 ms (16 with the game's own pass's polygons not drawn), the frame's work 34 → 23 ms (2.7M → 1.9M instructions at most; 22 ms, 1.8M); the machine waiting for the other 39-48 ms of work a frame (one frame 220 ms) → 16 ms. On the page (`scripts/web-armada-speed.mjs`, headless Chrome here): 17.1 frames a second either way on this machine, the worst frame's work 2.9M → 2.3M instructions (host) and 7.0M → 2.1M (guest, its wait), and Chrome's profile a third idle instead of 7%. With Chrome's CPU throttled three times (`THROTTLE=3`) neither keeps up (7-9 frames a second): there the game's transformations, run four times, the texture mapper's per-span setup and JEMM's page mapping (each EMS map four page-table entries and a reload of CR3) are what is left. What remained in the quarters, close up: the span setup (seg30DD) about a sixth of all the work (now done here, above), JEMM (CS 18h) an eighth, the transformations (45A2, 40B2, 4514, 36F8, 4720, 330F) a quarter; the cockpit as at 320x200, a sixth.

*Smoothness, <kbd>`</kbd> toggled* (the user, 2026-11-24: "toggle ` repeatedly does the min frame time over each mode stay the same ... especially if both ships are stopped"; `scripts/web-armada-speed.mjs` with `TOGGLE=key`: both pilots stopped close, ` pressed on both every 15 s eight times, each mode's 2 s windows apart, the first after each press dropped; the timing line now ends with the fewest frames in any wall-clock second). Headless Chrome on this machine, both pilots in it: 320x200 and 640x400 alike, 58.3 ms a frame (17.1 fps), 17 frames in every wall second (once 16, at 320x200), the worst frame 76-79 ms in both (the browser's own scheduling: it is there at 320x200 too). With Chrome's CPU throttled to half (`THROTTLE=2`), a slower machine: 320x200 17.0-17.1 fps, a window's worst frame typically 67-74 ms (the most 119-141), 17 frames in most wall seconds; 640x400 16.2 fps, a window's worst typically 113-121 ms (the most 145-150), 15 or 16 frames in nearly every wall second. So here the two are the same, and on a machine half as fast the 640x400 close-up drops about one frame a second.

*JEMM's page mapping, looked at and left as it is.* JEMM.OVL is Origin's own memory manager: it runs the game in V86 mode with its page tables, and EMS is its remapping of the page frame (a 24-page, 384 KB window here). Close up a frame makes about 180 INT 67h calls (counted at the CPU's interrupt entry): ~110 function 44h (one page: 58C1:00C8 through the game's cache of the window, 58C1:00FF without it), ~37 function 50h (58C1:0723: all 24 pages, called only when the game's own copy of the window shows one of them changed), ~20 of 47h/48h (save and restore the map) and ~12 VCPI calls. JEMM's own code, its ring 0 at CS 18h (16-bit; its live offsets are its file's + 330Fh for these routines), is 6-7% of the emulated instructions, most of it 50h's loop (18:70C1..718B): for each of the 24 entries four page-table entries copied from the handle's table, then a CR3 reload. No call is a no-op: the game already skips a request that changes nothing, and every map rewrites its page-table entries whole (their accessed and dirty bits back to clear) and empties the TLB, which the emulator then refills as the pages are touched. So the only way to take this work off the emulated CPU would be a host copy of JEMM's whole interrupt path: the CPU's entry from V86 and its IRETD, the bytes JEMM writes on its ring-0 stack and in its variables, the page tables and the flush, all reproduced exactly, for about 3% (50h) to 6% (all of them) of a close-up frame. That much of JEMM's and the emulator's protected-mode machinery redone by hand is not something to be sure of the way the polygon routine is, so it is not done (the user: "only proceed there if it's an absolutely bullet proof design and fix"). `WCNET_ARM_PROF` lists JEMM's hot places by 16 bytes.

## The strategy map one level out (src/cpu/wcnet_armada_hires.cpp, "the strategy map")

The user, 2026-11-22: "The next step after this will be done is for the high Rez mode to draw high Rez strategic maps. This means we may be able to fit more or all of the sector in one screen"; 2026-11-23: "Ideally everything is just zoomed out one level so that we don't need to size anything up"; "build one level out first: the game's own drawing four times, the grid thinned, the view kept inside the sector, the overview at double detail, and clicks that land where you see things. Forget whole sector view"; "If arrow keys are not bound it could be helpful"; "It's imperative the game is still playable and clicks don't act finicky"; "translation only happens on that top level map".

**How the map is drawn.** The strategy screen's map panel is x 105..301, y 15..151 of the 320x200 screen (197 x 137, its middle 203,83), drawn at one world unit a pixel. The view is the object at DS:[76CA] (ARMADA.EXE; [774A] in ARMADACD.EXE): its scroll at +42h/+44h is the world place of screen pixel 0, and the limits of its middle at +46h..+4Ch are the sector less half the panel (the game clamps its scroll to them, seg11DE:53F4). Each frame the screen's frame function (seg14C6:2DD2) draws the background, then the panel (`call 066D` at 3084: the grid, a line every 10 units and a darker one every 40, the jump lines, the selection's marker), then the registered objects (`call 449A` at 3090: each one's draw through its vtable +4: the planets, seg11DE:1086; the fleets, 2CE5; a route's markers, 2786; the overview, 14C6:44CD; the buttons, seg0B7E), then the dialogs and widgets, and at 311F the pointer and the copy to the screen. After the frame the update loop (14C6:0AA4 → 11DE:0927 → each object's update method) puts every object at its screen place for the next frame: a planet at its world place less the scroll with a hit box of ±5 (07B8), a fleet 12,−8 from its planet with ±3 (2892, called from 0B06), a route's marker beside its planet (23A0). Clicks are the game's own test of the pointer (DS:8EFA x in 640ths, 8EFC y) against those boxes.

**One level out.** A view twice as wide and tall at the same pixel size is four of the game's own panels side by side. With the picture on, the panel and the registered objects run four times a frame, each with the scroll shifted to one quarter of the doubled view and each object put where its own update would put it for that scroll (a planet from its world place, a fleet 12,−8 from its planet, a marker at its own offset from its planet; the pointer too, in that quarter's terms); only the map's own draws run in a quarter (the overview and the buttons not), and the panel's background is put back between them. Then everything runs once more as the game runs it. Nothing is scaled: every icon, line and circle is the game's own at its own pixel size, a 640x400 pixel each. Where the sector is smaller than the doubled view the view shows all of it that way.

- **Inside the sector** (the user: "are we allowed to peer below it?"): the doubled view's middle is kept within the game's limits less another half panel (or the sector's middle where the sector is narrower than the view), and the game's own scroll is put there, so both views share their middle.
- **The grid** (the user: "skip half the draws so the effective grid spacing on a screen is the same"): in a quarter only every other line is drawn, a line every 20 units and a darker one every 80, as far apart on the 640x400 screen as the game's are on its own.
- **Clicks.** While the picture is on, the game's own update (only from its update loop: the caller is checked) puts each planet at half its distance from the middle, where the 640x400 picture has it (the game's pointer is half the 640x400 one), and everything beside a planet at half its offset from it: a fleet (2892: 12,−8, so 6,−4) and a marker (23A0: its offset from 2361, a squadron 12 left of the planet in a column 8 apart, the other side's presence 12,8 below right; so 6 and 4). Hit boxes are the size of what is drawn: ±3 for a planet (its circle), ±2 for a fleet or a marker (its icon). The emulator keeps which planets are at their halved place now (the update's), and halves only what sits beside one of those. The game's own ±5 and ±3 at half the offsets overlapped and a click on a fleet took its planet; the user: "I always remebmer seeing ships and carriers right next to a planet". A marker's offset was at first left whole: the user's Phantom fighters, drawn 12 pixels left of their planet, answered a click 24 to the left ("the player must click most of the way towards the destination nav point to select the phantom at the source", 2026-11-23).
- **The wheel** (the user: "seamlessly zoom in (2x) and zoom out (normal) using wheel"): it steps between three levels, the game's own map (its own picture, doubled, its own clicks), one level out (the default) and further out (below), one a turn (a run of turns within 0.3 s, a trackpad's flick, is one step), about the pointer: the place under it stays under it as far as the view's limits allow (one level out a unit is a 640x400 pixel, on the game's own map two). The turn is taken at the next frame's entry, where the scroll and the pointer are the game's own (a wheel turn during the quarters would read a quarter's). Not while a dialog is over the map (view+2A4h, the Move Resources kind, or view+2ACh, the catalog's and the menu's, set). `!armwheel=1|-1` in a key script.
- **The overview's rectangle** (seg14C6:44CD) is the doubled view's: its half-sizes doubled for its computation (tripled further out), the result kept inside the overview; on the game's own map (the wheel up) the game's own. (Until 2026-11-23 it stayed the game's size one level out too: the hook was off in the game's own pass, where the overview is drawn.)
- **Further out** (the user: "would it be possible to do another zoom-out level at 3x3...rendering it 9 times then doing some kind of software downscale ... to reach the 2x2 we see today"; then "as long as we can zoom or click to disambiguate it seems worthwhile to provide that one last zoom out level--can really give a tactical view of the situation without making it too crowded"): three of the game's panels across and down in the picture of two, a world unit two thirds of a pixel. Not drawn nine times and shrunk: a picture shrunk by 1.5 loses every third row and column (circles broken, lines dotted) or, averaged, needs colours the palette has not got, and the icons shrink with it; and it would cost nine passes. Instead the same four passes put every planet at two thirds of its distance from the middle (the floor of 2/3 of each world place, so the places keep their distances as the view moves), each drawn by the game at its own size; the jump lines (seg11DE:1004) and the selection's bracket follow, being drawn from the planets' screen places; fleets and markers sit beside their planets at their own offsets as one level out. The grid alone is drawn from the scroll, and a quarter's scroll would give only a game's panel of it: so the emulator draws it (map_grid_draw) at the first of the game's grid calls in a quarter and skips the calls, a line every 30 units in the game's minor colour and every 120 in its major colour (at world places of the game's own lines), 20 and 80 pixels apart on the screen as at the other levels. The clicks (the update's places: the same distances, halved for the game's pointer), the view's limits (half as much again each way), the overview's rectangle (three times the game's) and the arrows (375 units a second, the same speed on the screen) go with it. Icons do not shrink, so a dense cluster is closer together; the wheel back in (about the pointer) or a click sorts it out. Checked natively with SAVE_2A8: every one of the 24 objects on KILLERBEE's turn and the 20 on HELLCATV's selects where it is drawn.
- **The arrow keys**, which the map does not use, move the view, 250 units a second one level out, 375 further out and 125 on the game's own map (the same speed on the screen), within the limits of each (the game's own glide to a place is stopped). They work with **High resolution (2x)** off too, and not while a dialog is over the map (the catalog's list takes Up and Down).
- **The pointer.** The mouse's interrupt writes the game's pointer (DS:8EFA, 8EFC) itself; one that moves it while a quarter has it in that quarter's terms is kept (the place is put back after the quarters only when the interrupt has not written a newer one: before, a move then was lost until the next).
- **The picture.** Each screen refresh the 640x400 picture is the game's screen doubled, and in the panel, wherever the screen has the game's own pass's pixel (of the newest frame, or the one before: the screen may show either), the doubled view there. What the frame draws over the panel besides the map (the BEGIN and DONE buttons, the dialogs, the map's pointer) is marked as it draws: in the game's own pass the panel is a marker colour while each such draw runs (and after the registered objects until the frame's end), so every pixel it writes is marked, a dialog's black over the map's black too, and the game's own pixel shows there. Only this panel is ever drawn more than once: the menus, dialogs and other screens are the game's own picture (the user: "I suspet menus and zoomed in screens are not going to need to be drawn 4x, probably only the map screens").

**The keys.** The game has keys for its buttons already (the user: "if we can do everything with extant shortcuts then we shouldn't have our own"; "if there are gaps we can add those"): each button's update (seg0B7E) compares the key just pressed (DS:852D, when it differs from the last one, DS:1DE1) with its own, F1 Menu, F2 Move, F3 Catalog, F4 Dock, F5 Planet menu, F6 Move resources, F7 Information, F8 Execute (which asks, Y or N); a dialog takes C for Cancel and D for Done, Esc too. Letters do nothing on the map. The gap was the arrows of three dialogs, a unit a click (repeating while the button is held), each dialog's mouse handler taking a row's left arrow at x C7..CD and its right at CF..D5, rows 8 high: Move Resources (seg0C92:11F5, rows from y 4C; left: a ton from the planet onto the ship), Dock (18EC, rows from 6E; left: one fighter undocked, right: one docked) and Move, which opens when a squadron is sent to another planet (0998, rows from 6E, REMAIN | MOVE; right: one more going; its own A is All). So the main row's **+** (= shifted or not) and **−** click the top row's arrows (the user: "maybe those could just do the top item....other ones you have to click"): + is more onto the ship (Move Resources' left, Dock's right) or more going (Move's right), − the other. The dialog is told by its mouse handler (the object at view+2A4h, its vtable +0Ch). From the map's frame (14C6:2DD2, the game's own flow) a press and a release go into the mouse's event queue (seg6A6C: a ring of 7-byte events, DS:6C2C start, 6C2E end, 6C30 head, 6C32 tail, 8ED8 the size; ARMADACD.EXE 6CB8.., 8F58), the dialog's handler takes them as a click there (its limits and all), and the pointer stays put. One a frame; the keys go on to the game, which has no use for them there, so the emulated keyboard repeats them while held (30 a second after half a second). Not the keypad's: the strategy screen's update takes a held keypad − (and keypad +, with the flag at DS:16AC) to set the side's record's +18h.

The picture is on while the map's frames come (each composes it) and goes a moment after they stop; it needs no faster CPU (the map runs as fast as it can; the flight's three times is the flight's alone). The CD build has the same code three paragraphs later; Proving Grounds has no map. Every place is checked by its bytes at the program's load (`ARMADA.EXE's strategy map can be drawn at 640x400` in the log; one that differs keeps the game's own map).
